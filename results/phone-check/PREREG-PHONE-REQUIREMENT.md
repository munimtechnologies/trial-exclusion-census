# Pre-registration: within telephone trials, does naming a phone go with naming hearing loss?

Written 2026-10-16, before any phrase was counted for this test. Only the twelve stratum sizes
and the four wording checks below were counted. Do not edit this file; additions go in a new
file and are labelled exploratory.
[prereg-phone-requirement.json](prereg-phone-requirement.json) holds the same design in a form
`phone_requirement.py` 0.1.0 is tested against. CC BY 4.0.
Made by MV2 of Munim, Inc. with AI agents.

## Why

[PHONE-MATCHED-RESULT.md](PHONE-MATCHED-RESULT.md) found that telephone-delivered no-phase
trials name a hearing wording 2.45 times (2.17-2.75) as often as other no-phase trials of the
same condition. [PHONE-HEARING-READ.md](PHONE-HEARING-READ.md) then read 20 of those hearing
mentions outside the VA. The hearing item itself names the phone in only 4 of 20, but 13 of the
20 trials name a phone somewhere in their criteria, against 5 of 20 other trials. That second
count was exploratory, and it is where this test comes from.

The question: **among telephone-delivered trials, do those whose eligibility criteria name a
phone (for example "must have access to a telephone") name a hearing wording more often than
those whose criteria don't, within the same condition, and more than they name phrases that have
nothing to do with hearing?** If so, the written phone requirement is where hearing exclusion
enters these trials. That is the step where captioned telephone, 711 relay, text or captioned
video could be offered instead.

## Design

- **Base:** interventional, no phase, all start years, interventions mention the telephone, lead
  sponsor not the VA, and own condition not cognitive, brain injury, stroke, hearing or vision:

  `AREA[StudyType]INTERVENTIONAL AND AREA[Phase]NA AND AREA[InterventionSearch]telephone AND NOT AREA[ConditionSearch](<OWN>) AND NOT AREA[LeadSponsorName](VA OR Veteran OR Veterans)`

  The VA is left out because its trials started this line of work; the sponsor clause is the
  one the S2 reading used.
- **Exposure (phone arm):** the base AND `AREA[EligibilityCriteria](phone OR telephone)`.
- **Comparator:** every other base trial of the same stratum (stratum count minus phone-arm
  count).
- **Strata,** disjoint, in priority order: the six of `phone_matched.py` (PTSD; substance use;
  depression; pain; cardiometabolic; other).
- **Phrases:** the twelve of `va_matched.py`: the cognitive, hearing and visual families; 8
  controls (pregnant, hepatitis, allergy, migraine, osteoporosis, claustrophobia, celiac,
  hernia), each counted without the trials of its own condition; and "informed consent" as a
  reference.
- **Measure:** the Mantel-Haenszel ratio of shares, phone arm over comparator, across the six
  strata, with the Greenland-Robins 95% interval. Unmatched and per-stratum ratios are reported
  beside it. A control with fewer than 5 phone-arm matches expected is left out of HP2 and S1.
- **Counts:** 12 stratum sizes and 144 phrase counts, 156 in all, each the `totalCount` of the
  query `phone_requirement.term()` builds, plus the 4 wording checks of
  `phone_requirement.wording_term()`, all on one day. On that day the six base sizes must add up
  to the whole base and the six phone-arm sizes to the `(phone OR telephone)` count.

### Wording check (counted 2026-10-16)

On the whole base, the registry search gave the same count for every wording:

| Criteria search | Trials |
|---|---|
| (none: the whole base) | 7,420 |
| `phone` | 1,890 |
| `telephone` | 1,890 |
| `(phone OR telephone)` | 1,890 |

So the search treats phone and telephone as one word here too. The test uses
`(phone OR telephone)` either way.

### Stratum sizes known before (counted 2026-10-16)

| Stratum | Phone named | All base trials |
|---|---|---|
| ptsd | 10 | 74 |
| substance use | 232 | 615 |
| depression | 94 | 389 |
| pain | 76 | 372 |
| cardiometabolic | 382 | 1,331 |
| other | 1,096 | 4,639 |
| sum | 1,890 | 7,420 |

The sums equal the whole-base counts above, which checks that the strata are disjoint and cover
the base. A phone is named in 25.5% of base trials, against 13 of the 20 (65%) in the
exploratory reading: that reading is why HP1 is expected to hold, and why HP2 is the real test.

## Hypotheses

- **HP1 (primary):** the hearing family's matched 95% interval lies above 1. Predicted: holds.
- **HP2 (primary, specificity):** the hearing family's matched ratio is larger than the visual
  family's and larger than every kept control's. Criteria that name a phone may simply be longer,
  which would lift every phrase; the controls are there to show that. If nothing set hearing
  apart, it would come first 1 time in (rivals + 1). This is a ranking, not a p-value. Predicted:
  holds, but this is the weaker prediction.
- **S1 (secondary):** the rank test of `va_matched.py`: the three families against the kept
  controls (exact one-sided rank sum).
- **S2 (secondary, reading):** read the first 20 trials, in the API's default order, of

  `<phone arm, whole base> AND AREA[EligibilityCriteria](<hearing family>)`

  (`phone_requirement.reading_term()`). For each trial, quote the item that names hearing and
  every item that matches `phone` (case-insensitive, so also telephone and smartphone), and mark:
  - **(a) requirement:** at least one *inclusion* item matches `phone`;
  - **(b) phone-tied:** the hearing item itself matches `phone|\bcalls?\b` (the rule of
    PHONE-HEARING-READ).

  Prediction: (a) in at least 12 of 20. This describes the trials; there is no test. Some of the
  20 may also be among the 20 read for PHONE-HEARING-READ; overlaps will be listed.

## How the results will be read

- HP1 and HP2 both hold: in telephone trials, a written phone requirement goes with hearing
  exclusion beyond a general fullness of criteria.
- HP1 holds and HP2 fails: criteria that name a phone name many things more often, and hearing is
  not singled out.
- HP1 fails: no evidence that the phone requirement carries the hearing link.
- A hearing item that names the phone itself ("hearing loss that prevents telephone calls") puts
  its trial in the phone arm by construction. If S2 marks (b) in at least 11 of 20, HP1 will be
  described as mostly the hearing items' own wording, not a separate requirement. The exploratory
  reading found 4 of 20.

## Limits known in advance

- Mentions anywhere in the criteria, not hand-checked exclusions (S2 reads 20 of them).
- "phone" also matches phone follow-up, phone screening and app access, so some phone-arm trials
  name a phone but don't require one. S2 (a) measures how many do.
- The comparison is only within telephone trials, so it can't say why telephone trials differ
  from other trials. Matching is on condition only, not on age, design or start year.
- The registry search expands synonyms, and counts change as records are added. One counting day,
  one reader (AI agents working for MV2), no person has checked the reading yet.
